Study overview and procedures

Introduction
You are being asked to take part in a research study. Please read this form carefully and take as much time as you need to decide. Ask the study doctor about anything you do not understand. No study tests or procedures will be done until you have signed this form.

Why is this study being done?
This is a research study. We want to find out how accurate and how safe the GlucoVarde 180 sensor is for adults with diabetes. The GlucoVarde 180 is a small glucose sensor placed under the skin of your upper arm for up to 180 days. It is an investigational device, which means that the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has not approved it. Isbrann Medical AS, the company that makes the sensor, pays for this study.

How many people will take part?
About 240 adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes will take part at up to 12 study sites in the United States.

How long will I be in the study?
You will take part in the study for about 7 months and come to 6 clinic visits, plus 3 phone calls between visits.

What will happen during the study?
At the screening visit we will ask about your health and take a small blood sample, and a pregnancy test if you can become pregnant.
At the insertion visit, the study doctor will numb a small area of your upper arm, make a cut about 5 mm long and place the sensor under the skin. You will wear a small transmitter over the sensor, held by an adhesive patch, and see your glucose readings in the study app.
You will come to the clinic for 3 long visits of about 8 hours. A nurse will place a small tube (IV) in a vein in your arm and take a blood sample every 15 minutes to compare with the sensor readings.
At the day 180 visit, the study doctor will numb the area again and take the sensor out through a small cut. The risks of these procedures are explained in the section on risks.

Which parts are experimental?
The sensor, the procedures to place and remove it, and the long clinic visits are done only for this research. Finger-stick blood sugar checks are regular diabetes care.

Can I be taken out of the study?
The study doctor may take you out of the study without your consent if staying in would be unsafe for you, if you become pregnant, if you need an MRI scan or if the study is stopped. If this happens, the sensor will be removed at a removal visit.
