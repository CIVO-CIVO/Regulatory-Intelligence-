Risks, benefits and alternatives

What are the risks of placing and removing the sensor?
Placing and removing the sensor can cause pain or tenderness for a few days (about 1 in 5 people), bruising (about 1 in 7 people) and redness around the cut (about 1 in 17 people). Infection at the insertion site happens in fewer than 1 in 500 people and is treated with antibiotics. In about 1 in 40 people the sensor is hard to remove, and the study doctor needs to make a second small cut. You will have a small scar where the sensor was placed.

What are the risks of wearing the transmitter?
The adhesive patch that holds the transmitter can make your skin red, itchy or sore (about 1 in 6 people). Changing the patch every day and moving it slightly helps.

What are the risks of the long clinic visits?
The IV line and the many blood samples can cause bruising, pain and, rarely, fainting. Over the 3 long visits we will take about 300 mL of blood in total, a little more than one cup.

Are there other risks?
The sensor readings may be wrong. If you treat a high or low glucose based on a wrong reading, your blood sugar could go too low or too high. This is why you must check with a finger stick before any treatment decision. You must not have an MRI scan while the sensor is in your arm, and you should tell any doctor who treats you that you have the sensor. There may also be risks that are not known yet.

What if I am hurt in the study?
If you are hurt because of the study, Isbrann Medical AS may help with some of the costs. Talk to the study doctor if this happens.

Will I benefit from being in the study?
The GlucoVarde 180 is safe and gives more accurate readings than the continuous glucose monitor you wear on your skin today. You will also not need to change a sensor every 10 to 14 days. What we learn may help other people with diabetes in the future.

What other choices do I have?
You do not have to take part in this study to manage your diabetes. Other choices include finger-stick meters and continuous glucose monitors that FDA has already cleared, which you wear on the skin. Your own doctor can talk with you about these choices.
